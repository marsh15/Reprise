# Interview notes

Use these as prompts for your own account, not a script of personal contributions. The implementation and offline evidence are documented through M6; live-provider acceptance and model selection remain blocked. `gpt-6-luna` is still the provisional default, not a benchmark winner.

## Design and tradeoffs

**What problem shaped the architecture?** Reprise answers questions about past conversations and changing information. It records answers as dated observations and supplies relevant history before generation. Code handles calendar calculations, retrieval, source identities and resource limits; one model handles language, search decisions and synthesis. This division reduces some avoidable errors, but it does not make the model's conclusions deterministic or guarantee correctness. References: [turn service](../src/server/turns/service.ts), [context](../src/server/memory/context.ts).

**Why one model and one tool?** The model has `web_search`; recall is code, not another model tool. A custom loop makes budgets, evidence and termination visible without a router, multi-agent system or second model verifier. Each additional model call would add complexity and latency without a demonstrated need in this scope. Whether this single-model design performs well in practice still needs live evaluation. References: [loop](../src/server/agent/runTurn.ts), [tool contract](../src/server/agent/modelStep.ts).

**How does the loop stop?** `createRunTurn` and `researchRound` allow at most three tool-enabled rounds, five queries per round and ten per turn, with concurrent independent searches, an eight-second search bound and a 25-second research deadline. Results are capped at five per query and excerpts at 1,500 characters. Once research is exhausted, a tool-disabled final step is instructed to disclose what could not be verified. The research deadline is not an end-to-end answer timeout; cancellation is separate. References: [budgets](../src/server/agent/budget.ts), [research round](../src/server/agent/researchRound.ts).

**Why SQLite and FTS, not vectors or summaries?** A local single-user app can store raw turns and query local dates plus FTS5 keywords without a separate database or embedding pipeline. Raw questions and answers remain the record; summaries could omit or alter details. Keyword recall misses paraphrases and vague references, and bounded context can omit relevant records. Measure those failures first, consider synonym expansion, then embeddings if warranted; vector similarity would introduce its own false matches and operational costs. SQLite's `node:sqlite` avoids a separate native addon but requires the supported Node runtime. References: [database](../src/server/db/db.ts), [schema/FTS](../src/server/db/schema.ts), [recall](../src/server/memory/recall.ts).

**How are dates resolved?** `resolveDates` uses the supplied timezone and calendar arithmetic, including DST boundaries, for supported English phrases. Ambiguous “last Tuesday” yields two candidates. `recall` prefers a candidate with a topic match, otherwise the most recent candidate with recorded turns; it records alternatives, assumptions and topic mismatches. No matching history is reported as such. The rules do not understand every expression, and choosing a candidate is not proof of the user's intent. References: [resolver](../src/server/time/resolve.ts), [selection](../src/server/memory/recall.ts).

**What prevents false memory?** The recall packet contains bounded raw turns, relevant sources, current context, history start and date assumptions. The prompt instructs abstention when evidence is missing; empty-history cases have offline tests. This limits the evidence supplied, but the model can still invent or misinterpret a memory, and retrieval can miss one. A failed recall returns a memory-unavailable note rather than partial history. Live compliance is unmeasured. References: [prompt](../src/server/agent/prompt.ts), [memory scenarios](../evals/memoryScenarios.ts).

**What is the evidence ledger?** SQLite stores messages, searches, source observations and citation records. Raw historical messages establish what was said, not that it was true. Original source IDs, excerpts and retrieval dates persist; fresh searches create new observations. Demo/fixture provenance is labelled. Publication, retrieval, discussion and effective dates have different meanings; a published date alone does not establish when a price took effect. References: [ledger persistence](../src/server/db/repo.ts), [observation registry](../src/server/agent/sourceRegistry.ts).

**How do you know a price changed?** A source explicitly stating and dating a change can support a verified change within its scope. Different historical and current observations support an observed difference, not the date or cause of a change; missing evidence or conflict may leave it unverifiable. Compare the same product and tier, seats, units, currency, billing commitment, discounts and effective dates. The model is instructed to do this comparison; code does not compute a structured price history, and live semantic performance is untested. References: [change rules](../src/server/agent/prompt.ts), [recheck scenarios](../evals/recheckScenarios.ts).

**What do citations actually verify?** `createSourceRegistry` assigns markers to normalized URLs and preserves separate observations. `checkCitations` marks unknown markers invalid and checks recognizable numerical/date tokens against the combined excerpts cited in a sentence; claims without such tokens are unchecked. A matching number under the wrong plan can pass, and correct derived arithmetic can be marked unverified. One URL can share a marker across dates, so a marker does not identify the supporting observation. The UI shows the observations and explains this limitation. Neither the meaning, source quality, nor the supporting date is verified by token matching. References: [checker](../src/server/agent/citations.ts), [citation UI](../src/client/components/Citation.tsx).

## Reliability and evidence

**Streaming and recovery.** The server streams SSE events, can reset a provisional draft when a model step becomes a tool call, and persists complete, failed or interrupted attempts. Stop uses abort signals; disconnects can interrupt work. The client retains an unacknowledged request identity for recovery, distinguishes a lost terminal event from a confirmed failure, and refreshes the authoritative transcript. Acknowledged incomplete attempts can be retried against the saved user message; the earlier attempt remains visible. Browser storage failure limits refresh recovery of an unacknowledged request. References: [client reconciliation](../src/client/components/Thread.tsx), [SSE parser](../src/client/api.ts), [process recovery tests](../tests/integration/server.test.ts).

**Transactions and idempotency.** `createTurnService.prepare` checks the client message ID and rejects conflicting reuse; completed requests replay and retry targets are restricted to the latest incomplete user message. An in-process active-turn map prevents concurrent turns per conversation in this process. `openDatabase` uses synchronous `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transactions; `repo` atomically creates a conversation/user row and separately saves an assistant attempt with its evidence. This is not an atomic transaction across network generation, nor a distributed exactly-once guarantee. Distinct retry IDs use the same unique request-ID column on assistant attempts; active IDs are reserved by the existing turn map until persistence. Replaying a saved retry returns that exact attempt. Deletion waits for an active turn in its own conversation to stop; a source deleted from another conversation becomes an unavailable citation without discarding the new answer. A crash before attempt persistence can lose in-flight work; this is not exactly-once generation. References: [service](../src/server/turns/service.ts), [repository](../src/server/db/repo.ts), [regressions](../tests/integration/turns.test.ts).

**What has been proved?** M6 records 339/339 offline tests and 23/23 scripted mock scenarios on Node 24 and 26, plus builds and type checks. M5 records the broader touch, responsive and accessibility checks; M6 rechecks representative browser/process flows. Unit/integration tests exercise code and persistence; scripted mocks prove controlled orchestration and assertions, not model reasoning. Synthetic-search replay, recorded-search replay, mixed replay and fully live runs are distinct evidence categories; cassettes make inputs comparable, not necessarily realistic. Human semantic review is separate. See the checkpoints for exact scope and limitations. References: [runner](../evals/execute.ts), [report](../evals/report.ts), [evaluation limits](../evals/README.md).

**What remains unknown?** Real tool choice, query quality, memory interpretation, injection resistance, price comparison, semantic grounding, conversational quality, latency and cost remain unmeasured. OpenAI and Tavily credentials were absent; an optional OpenRouter request returned 402 before generation, and the Terra Chat Completions tool pairing has a guard. No live winner or cheap-model success is established. Live acceptance needs compatible provider access, real search, controlled comparisons and human review; unresolved provider compatibility or credit issues may also require decisions. References: [compatibility guard](../src/server/llm/client.ts), [smoke](../scripts/smoke-llm.ts).

**How would you debug a bad answer?** Use the streamed trace ID to identify the response, and message/conversation IDs to inspect the ledger. Runtime logs cover startup and recall failures; detailed model requests, recalled packets and event traces are captured by the evaluation runner, not a production trace archive. Inspect those traces, assumptions, search outcomes, excerpts, dates and citation states. Reproduce with a frozen clock and appropriate cassette; distinguish a retrieval miss, search failure, checker limitation and model interpretation failure. Never treat a synthetic reproduction as proof of live behavior. References: [trace capture](../evals/execute.ts), [logging](../src/server/log.ts).

## Judgment and ownership

**Hard decisions.** Keeping recall deterministic trades paraphrase coverage for inspectable records. Treating two observations as a difference rather than a dated change avoids inventing history but can yield an inconclusive answer. A lightweight citation check is fast and transparent but can create false confidence if called semantic verification. Bounded research limits runaway work but can leave a question unresolved. These are deliberate tradeoffs, not universal guarantees. References: [decisions](adr/), [design](../DESIGN.md).

**Scale.** The local single-user design has no authentication, uses a process-local active-turn map and faces SQLite's single-writer contention and growing FTS/history costs. A multi-user deployment would need authorization and tenant isolation, durable coordination or a queue, database scaling and retention controls. Consider a managed relational store and stronger retrieval only when measured workload and recall failures justify them. References: [active map](../src/server/turns/service.ts), [SQLite transactions](../src/server/db/db.ts).

**Another week.** First unblock live provider and Tavily access, run controlled evaluations and review failures. Then consider structured price observations with units and dates, archived historical evidence, targeted paraphrase retrieval, source trust and memory correction/deletion controls. Prioritize from observed failures rather than assuming these additions solve them. References: [accepted scope](PLAN.md#s-explicit-non-goals).

**AI contribution.** AI assisted implementation and documentation. The plan records architectural decisions and milestone approval gates; it does not independently establish who personally wrote, approved, tested or reviewed each line. The owner should describe only their actual decisions, approvals, review and hands-on contributions, and explain any part they can defend. Do not repeat the plan's unsupported claim that the owner reviewed every module. References: [approved decisions and operating rules](PLAN.md).

- `src/server/turns/service.ts`: request identity, per-conversation activity, recall, persistence, replay and cancellation.
- `src/server/time/resolve.ts`, `src/server/memory/recall.ts` and `context.ts`: timezone-aware candidates, FTS/date selection and bounded context.

- `evals/` and `docs/M4-CHECKPOINT.md`, `docs/M5-CHECKPOINT.md`: scenario execution, evidence categories and recorded verification boundaries.
